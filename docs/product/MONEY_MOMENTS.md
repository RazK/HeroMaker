# Money moments: where people want to pay

Raz, 2026-11-22: "Make sure we don't spend time on things that don't bring money.
Find where the most value for users is… the moments people want to spend money,
and point us there: in the site's design, the content, the campaigns, everything."

This page is the filter for every request, every design and every campaign.
Each moment below is a hypothesis until data says otherwise (`HYPOTHESES.md`).

## The five moments

| # | Moment | What the person feels | What we offer right then |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The reveal**: the parent and child see the drawing painted, then moving | "Wow, it's really theirs" | Keep it, make the 3D hero, make another |
| 2 | **The child's reaction**: the child shouts, dances, shows everyone | Pride, joy | Share it; send it to grandma |
| 3 | **"I want to keep this"**: the wow fades unless it becomes a thing | Fear of losing it | The printed toy, a keepsake video, a print |
| 4 | **The gift**: a birthday, a holiday (Hanukkah is in December), grandparents | "A gift nobody else can give" | A gift made from the child's own drawing |
| 5 | **Home from class**: the child brings a hero home from a class or event | Curiosity, then the reveal again | Open it at home; make another; print it |

## The rule

Before any work: **which moment does it serve, and how soon does it bring money?**
None, or "someday" → later, or no. The product manager says so out loud.

## What this means now

- **Pay at the peak, not before it.** Today people pay before they see anything.
  Let the first wow (the painting) come first, then ask (experiment E-006).
- **The site shows the reveal**, not the machinery: drawing → painting → moving hero.
- **Content and ads show real reactions** (with consent), and the gift angle in December.
- **Sell a keepsake**: the printed toy is the natural answer to moment 3.
- **Classes and events are the way in** (moment 5); the family is the buyer.

## Not money moments (do less, or later)

More games, more polish on screens people already like, gallery-order tests,
new animations for their own sake. Games keep a child busy; they do not make a
parent pay. Do them only when a money moment needs them.
