# Finding product-market fit

A living doc (Raz asked for it, 2026-11-23): where is the real pull, who pays,
which business direction answers a real need. Facts are cited; everything else
is marked an assumption (operating kit: never manufacture market research).

## What the market already proves (facts, with sources)

1. **Parents pay a premium for a PHYSICAL keepsake of their child's drawing.**
   Budsies turns a drawing into a ~16" plush for about **$69-115**, ~2 months
   turnaround, with a $20 "redraw" add-on. A whole business on one drawing →
   one physical object. [Budsies reviews]
2. **The "save my kid's art" keepsake market is large and crowded.** Artkive
   claims **500k+ families and 21M art pieces** turned into books (~$149) and
   framed prints; rivals: Keepy, Keepbox, Plum Print, Canvsly, Art My Kid
   Made, Artsonia. Parents clearly pay to keep and gift kids' art. [Artkive,
   TechCrunch]
3. **Animating a drawing is commoditised and nearly free.** Meta's Animated
   Drawings is free and open-source; many apps do it (Drawings Alive,
   SketchPop, Animate my Drawing, DoodleTale, Keepbox). **"It comes alive" is
   table stakes, not a moat.** [Meta Animated Drawings; app listings]
4. **The school channel that works is free-to-school, parents-buy.** Artsonia
   is free for schools and families and monetises through OPTIONAL keepsake
   products; the art program keeps ~20%. Big installed base, but revenue per
   school is modest ("considerably less than Art to Remember", the physical
   fundraiser). Needs parent consent (COPPA/FERPA). [Artsonia; TechLearning]

## What this means for us

- **The money is in the KEEPSAKE and the GIFT, not the animation.** Budsies
  and Artkive prove parents pay $70-150 for something tangible from their
  child's art. Nobody gets rich selling the animation, because it is free
  elsewhere.
- **Our wedge is the LOOP no competitor has in one place:** the magic reveal →
  a 3D hero the child can PLAY with → a SHARE video the family passes around →
  an optional printed TOY to keep. Budsies/Artkive give the keepsake but no
  play or share; the animation apps give a clip but nothing to keep. The share
  is also our cheapest acquisition (word of mouth, the operating plan's spine).
- **Classes and events are a funnel, not the payer.** The proven model
  (Artsonia) is free to the school; the PARENT buys later. So our bet should be
  classes/events free at the top, and the family buying the keepsake at home —
  not schools paying much.

## The PMF bet (assumption — to be tested)

> The strongest pull is **a family buying a keepsake of their child's
> drawing-hero** — the digital "bring it to life" to catch the moment cheaply
> (₪10-39), and the **printed toy as the premium** (₪149) that matches the
> proven $70-150 willingness-to-pay. Classes and events (Schneider, a school)
> are the free, viral top of the funnel.

This **validates** our current thesis (keepsake + gift; classes as the way in)
and adds one warning: do not position or price as "animate your drawing" — that
is a race to free. Lead with the experience, the share and the thing you keep.

## The riskiest question (test first)

**Will parents pay for the DIGITAL hero, or only for the PHYSICAL toy?** Every
proven keepsake business above is physical. Digital drawing-animation is free.
So our ₪10-39 digital price is the real unknown. Tests already planned read
exactly this:
- "Pay after the painting" (E-006 / FREE_PAINTING) — do they pay for the
  digital hero once they have seen the reveal?
- The toy fake door (H-036, ₪149) — how many want the physical keepsake?
- Schneider + a class (P-001) — do families buy at home afterwards (the
  Artsonia funnel)?

Read these three before spending on acquisition. They tell us whether PMF is
digital, physical, or both.
