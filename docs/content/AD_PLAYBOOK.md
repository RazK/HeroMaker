# HeroMaker ad playbook (Tom's seminar notes, 24 Nov 2026)

Raz asked for a measured hour of study before starting the ad again. The
sources are three research reports plus a second round (competitor ads,
second-by-second teardowns), listed at the end of each section. **[P]** marks
platform data, **[V]** vendor or practitioner data, **[O]** opinion.

## 1. The brief comes before the storyboard

Every ad starts with these five lines. If one is missing, nothing gets drawn.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A paid ad (direct response). Explainers ("how it works") live on the site. |
| **For whom** | A parent of a 4–9-year-old who draws, scrolling Instagram or Facebook in the evening. |
| **One job** | The parent stops, feels "that's MY kid's drawing, alive", and taps *Make a hero*. |
| **Where** | Meta Reels and Stories at 9:16 (and TikTok later). Most views have sound on, but the ad must also work muted. |
| **Measure** | Hook rate (3 s plays ÷ impressions, about 25% is average and 30% or more is good), then hold rate, CTR, cost per hero started, and paid heroes. |

## 2. What wins in this category (10 references)

- **IKEA Soft Toys for Education (2015).** Children's drawings became plush toys, and the wonky eyes were kept. The press ran the drawing next to the toy.
- **Budsies, "Ben" (2022).** "Wait wait wait", then he runs to hold the plush up beside his drawing on the wall. Ownership recognition is the peak.
- **Meta Animated Drawings.** 6.7M drawings uploaded with no paid media. Dancing is the motion everyone shares. Its output is flat 2D, while ours is painted, 3D and playable.
- **Samsung, "A Friend for Christmas" (2025).** An adult helps, the child stays the author. Parents believe imagination peaks around age 6.
- **Crayola × Zach King (2015).** "A hero draws near": the hero steps out of the page. The seamless magic transition works with the sound off.
- **instagr.ai and Tom Curtis reels.** The drawing stays pinned in a corner the whole time, so no words are needed. The child's "mistakes" are the delight.
- **MyHeritage Deep Nostalgia.** 26M animations in 11 days. The first blink of a loved still image is the hook.
- **Remini, "Me seeing me".** About 9 s, split screen from frame 1, the same beat repeated three times.
- **Wonderbly.** Proof that it's personal is the hook: the child's own name in the book. Parent-voice quotes, around 70 new creatives a week.
- **Tonies, Yoto, Lovevery.** The most-iterated winners run 9–15 s, not 40 s testimonials. One action by the child, an instant magic response, the child dances. Two-beat text: "From first words / to tiny dancer."

**What this changes for us.**

1. **The winners almost never show the process.** The making (upload, painting, progress) is at most 2–3 s, or skipped. My rejected storyboard spent half the film on upload and making. That was an explainer, not an ad.
2. **The best structure for us is PhotoRoom/Pixelcut:** a real drawing on a real table → a real hand holds the phone over it → **the hero, alive, next to the drawing**. The real object stays in frame.
3. **9–12 s of content plus a 2–4 s card.** Never longer.
4. **One caption can carry the whole ad** (Lovevery, Remini). Two lines at most.
5. **Lo-fi beats polished in the first second.** A real hand and a phone camera feel like the feed; a cinematic opening feels like an ad, and people scroll.

### Competitors: who is advertising "a child's drawing comes to life"

**The market.** The direct AI competitors are small and barely buy ads.
- Drawings Alive: 5K+ installs. A subscription from $9.99 a month, 6-second videos, no play.
- AniMagic: about 100 followers. It never shows the drawing.
- Meta Animated Drawings: flat 2D.
- Arta: one ad with 7.8M plays.

The big reach came from people and handmade work: Tom Curtis (6.6M), Budsies on Insider (4.8M), CrunchLabs (5.2M), and a dad who 3D-printed his son's drawing (1.4M).

**What I saw in their ads myself.**
- **Arta (7.8M, 18 s).** A real hand-held notebook, then "SCANNING… / TRANSFORMING… / RESULT", three drawings in 15 s, then an App Store card. It works, but the result changes the drawing: the frog gets a raincoat and comes out as a different character.
- **Drawings Alive, giraffe (6 s).** A hand holds the drawing in the foreground with the 3D character blurred behind it. At 1.5 s the focus pulls from the paper to the character, which turns its head and smiles. **The best device I saw: the drawing and the result in one shot, without a split.**
- **Drawings Alive, "Moms, you HAVE to try this".** A surprised parent at the top, then the drawing on a torn-paper strip above the animation below. The ad shows cinematic multi-character scenes the app doesn't produce, which is an honesty gap.

**The white space we can own.**
1. **"Every line kept."** The most-loved work keeps the wrong anatomy (Curtis; "doesn't change a single stroke"), while AI apps "fix" it. No ad proves the extra leg survived.
2. **Not a video: a hero the child plays with.** Every competitor ends on a 6-second clip or a dance loop.
3. **One child, not a gallery.**
4. **Paper → 3D → play → a printed figure in the hand.** Nobody closes that loop. (Printing is later, with Nir.)
5. **Honesty.** Unedited footage of the real output, against competitors who show what they can't deliver.

**Hooks worth testing (from the competitor research).**
- "He drew 5 legs. It still has 5 legs."
- A focus pull from the paper to the hero.
- "She drew this at breakfast." Shown over play, with the drawing in the corner.
- The fridge: from the magnet to the screen.

### Patterns we adopt

1. **Drawing and result together.** The real crayon drawing stays in frame, or in a corner, the whole time.
2. **The "after" first.** The transformation appears in seconds 0–3, with no setup and no UI in the hook.
3. **The first motion of a still thing is the hook.** The hero blinks, then steps off the paper.
4. **Keep the child's imperfections.** Show the quirk surviving into the 3D hero. That is what "my kid's" looks like.
5. **The peak is recognition.** The phone held up next to the drawing on the fridge: "that's mine."
6. **The parent is the quiet hero and the buyer.** The CTA is something the parent does for the child.
7. **9–15 s.** Cut 6 s and 9 s versions from the master.
8. **Muted grammar.** Two to four text cards of three to six words each, in the safe middle band, with music.
9. **Repeat one beat across 2–3 different drawings.** That implies "any drawing works".
10. **End on joyful play, not a feature list.** Then a short end card.

## 3. Craft rules (Meta and TikTok)

- **Hook.** The value or product is visible within 3 s, and the hook resolves by 6 s. People spend about 1.7 s per mobile feed item. [P]
- **No logo first.** No slow fade-in, no preamble.
- **Structure.** Hook (0–3 s), then proof (the real app), then payoff (the child's joy), then CTA. Cut every 2–3 s, and at least twice in the first 5 s for apps. [P Google]
- **Sound.** Over 75% of Reels views are sound-on. [P] Design for both: the ad must be fully understood muted, and better with sound.
  - Use licence-safe music only (Meta Sound Collection, TikTok Commercial Music Library, or a library licence).
  - Put a sound effect on every tap or sparkle.
- **Supers.**
  - One idea per card, two lines at most, 3–6 words.
  - 48–75 px bold at 1080 wide, high contrast.
- **Safe zones.** On 1080×1920 Reels and Stories, keep clear the top 14% (about 270 px), the bottom 35% (about 672 px) and 6% on each side (about 65 px). TikTok adds a right-hand button column. **Critical text goes in the 15–60% band.**
- **Showing the app.**
  - Real current UI, close and legible.
  - The key tap and result run at real speed. Boring waits are jump-cut with an honest time card.
  - No fake system UI (Meta's "non-existent functionality" rule).
- **Brand and CTA.**
  - The product is visible by about 2–3 s. The name is shown once.
  - The end card shows the icon and name, one promise, and "Make a hero". The platform CTA button is set too.
  - The landing page must keep the same promise.
- **Testing on a small budget.**
  1. Run 3–4 genuinely different concepts.
  2. On the winner, change only the first 3 s.
  3. Read hook rate → hold rate → CTR → cost per start → paid.
  4. Write the decision rule down before launch.
  - About 5% of creatives win, so volume beats polish.

### Editing and sound (second round of study)
- **Retention.** A cliff in the first seconds means the opening didn't deliver its promise. A slow, steady slide means dead moments: cut them, don't speed them up. A visual change about every 2–4 s (a cut, a zoom, a new caption) holds attention. This is vendor data, so treat it as directional.
- **Sound to the cut.**
  - The whoosh starts 2–4 frames *before* the cut. The impact lands on the frame of the change, or 1–3 frames after.
  - The transformation gets a short riser plus a light impact.
  - Each text card gets a soft tick.
  - Don't use the same whoosh on every cut, or it sounds like a template.
- **Captions.**
  - 3–5 words per card, at least 0.8–1.2 s on screen.
  - An accent colour on the key word works better than an underline or box.
  - Lower-middle of the frame (inside the 15–60% band), never on top of the subject.

## 4. What moves the parent

- **Keepsake.** 74% of parents keep their children's art, and a third wish they had kept more. Many throw drawings away and feel guilty about it. [YouGov, BIC] So the line is "the drawing doesn't end up in a drawer; it becomes their hero".
- **Pride and ownership.** Children value what they made from about age 5 (the "IKEA effect"). [Cognition 2018]
- **Creative screen time.** Parents feel guilty about passive screen time. "Screen time they create" is fair. We do not claim "educational" or "screen-free".
- **Gifts.** Grandparents spend on grandchildren, and 85% spend on gifts and treats. [AARP 2026] Use it as a separate cut or a 1-second beat, not the core.
- **Price friction is low; trust is the friction.**
  - Show the real output.
  - Say "no ads, no subscription" only if that is true.
  - Show one honest full price, including VAT, with no "only".

## 5. Rules we must keep (an AI cast, children, Israel)

1. **Every hero frame is real app output.** An "AI" label does not fix a misleading result. [ASA; Meta unrealistic outcomes; Israeli Consumer Protection Law s.2]
2. **The AI family is never presented as real customers.** No reviews, star ratings or "Sarah from Haifa". Mark it as a dramatization with AI characters. [FTC fake-reviews rule 2024; Endorsement Guides 255.2(c); EU AI Act Art. 50 from 2 Aug 2026]
3. **Expect platform AI labels.** Meta puts "AI info" next to Sponsored for photorealistic AI people; TikTok requires its AIGC label. A slightly stylised cast also softens the "inauthentic" penalty. [NIQ 2024]
4. **Speak to the parent, never the child.** No "ask Mom", no "all your friends have one". Target adults and exclude under-18s, which is the definition of a minor in Israel's advertising-to-minors regulations. [CAP 5.4.2; Israeli regulations, 1991]
5. **Don't fake speed.** Show the making beat or an honest time jump ("a few minutes later"). No "instantly". [TikTok; Meta]
6. **Price.** One full figure including VAT, with no "only" or "just" and no hidden extras. [Israeli Consumer Protection Law ss.14C/17D]
7. **Children's data.** A real child's drawing, face or name in an ad needs written parental consent. Our own drawing (Violet) avoids that. [COPPA 2025; GDPR Art. 8; Israel Amendment 13]
8. **Meta copy.** Never imply personal attributes ("Are you a mom of a 6-year-old?"). Use "For kids 4–9" instead.
9. **Keep the AI child in ordinary, safe, fully clothed situations.**

## 6. Per-ad checklist

- [ ] The brief's five lines are written.
- [ ] 9:16, 15 s master, with 6–9 s cutdowns.
- [ ] 0–3 s: the drawing and the hero coming alive. The first frame is not a logo.
- [ ] The real drawing is in frame or in a corner throughout. The hero is real app output.
- [ ] A cut every 2–3 s. The app (real UI) appears by about 5 s.
- [ ] The peak: the child recognises her hero. The end: play, then the end card.
- [ ] 2–4 supers of 3–6 words, all inside the 15–60% band.
- [ ] Understood muted. Licence-safe music and SFX make it better with sound.
- [ ] Honest making time. No fake UI. Full price if stated.
- [ ] AI cast labelled, never a "customer". Parent-directed copy.
- [ ] A hook variant planned (2–3 different first 3 s).
